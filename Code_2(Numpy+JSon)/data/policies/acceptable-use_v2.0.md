# Acceptable Use Policy v2.0
**Effective Date:** December 1, 2025
**Supersedes:** v1.2
**Superseded By:** None (current)

## 1. Purpose
To define appropriate use of company technology resources, protect those resources from misuse, and ensure compliance with legal, regulatory, and ethical standards in an evolving digital workplace.

## 2. Scope
This policy applies to all workforce members (employees, contractors, consultants, temporaries, volunteers, interns, and affiliates) and all company technology resources, including:
- Company-owned devices (desktops, laptops, mobile devices, servers)
- Personal devices used for company work (under BYOD policy)
- Company networks, systems, applications, and data
- Cloud services and third-party platforms used for company business
- Communication systems (email, messaging, video conferencing, telephony)
- Social media accounts representing the company
- Any technology resource accessing company data or networks

## 3. General Use Principles
Company technology resources are provided primarily to support business operations. Limited incidental personal use is permitted when it:
- Does not interfere with job responsibilities or productivity
- Does not consume excessive system resources or bandwidth
- Does not violate any provision of this policy
- Does not expose the company to unnecessary risk or liability
- Occurs primarily during designated breaks or outside scheduled work hours

## 4. Permitted Activities
- Accessing information and resources necessary for job performance
- Communication with colleagues, clients, partners, and vendors for business purposes
- Use of company-approved software, services, and cloud platforms
- Limited personal communications and web browsing during breaks
- Online training, certification, and professional development activities
- Use of company resources for approved community service or volunteering
- Access to disability accommodations and assistive technologies as approved

## 5. Prohibited Activities

### 5.1 Illegal and Unlawful Activities
- Any activity that violates applicable laws, regulations, or legal requirements
- Unauthorized access to computer systems, networks, or data (hacking)
- Creation, distribution, or use of malicious software (viruses, ransomware, spyware, etc.)
- Copyright infringement, software piracy, or violation of intellectual property rights
- Possession, creation, or distribution of illegal materials as defined by law
- Conducting illegal business activities using company resources

### 5.2 Security and Integrity Violations
- Sharing passwords, PINs, biometric data, or other authentication credentials
- Attempting to bypass, disable, or circumvent security controls or monitoring
- Connecting unauthorized or unapproved devices to company networks
- Installing unauthorized software, hardware, or firmware on company devices
- Disabling, tampering with, or interfering with security software (antivirus, firewall, etc.)
- Attempting to access systems, networks, or data without proper authorization
- Introducing malicious code or conducting security testing without explicit authorization
- Physical tampering with company technology equipment

### 5.3 Harassment and Discrimination
- Creating, accessing, distributing, or storing harassing, intimidating, or threatening content
- Creating, accessing, distributing, or storing discriminatory content based on protected characteristics
- Engaging in cyberbullying, online harassment, or creating hostile work environments digitally
- Using company resources to stalk, intimidate, or retaliate against individuals
- Creating or distributing content that creates a hostile work environment

### 5.4 Inappropriate and Offensive Content
- Creating, accessing, distributing, or storing obscene, pornographic, or sexually explicit material
- Creating, accessing, distributing, or storing excessively violent or graphic content
- Creating, accessing, distributing, or storing content promoting illegal activities
- Creating, accessing, distributing, or storing content that could reasonably offend coworkers
- Engaging in sexting or sharing sexually suggestive content via company resources

### 5.5 Resource Abuse and Mismanagement
- Excessive personal use that interferes with job responsibilities or duties
- Operating personal business activities or side hustles using company resources
- Unauthorized fundraising, solicitation, or political campaigning using company resources
- Streaming entertainment content, gaming, or other high-bandwidth personal activities
- Cryptocurrency mining, blockchain operations, or other resource-intensive computations
- Denial of service activities or attempts to overwhelm system resources
- Unauthorized use of company resources for external consulting or employment

### 5.6 Data and Information Violations
- Unauthorized copying, sharing, transmission, or removal of company data
- Sending confidential or sensitive information via unsecured or unauthorized channels
- Using company resources to process, store, or transmit personal data without authorization
- Violating data privacy policies, confidentiality agreements, or data handling procedures
- Accessing, viewing, or distributing confidential information without business need
- Improper disposal or destruction of company data or media containing company data
- Attempting to conceal or obfuscate unauthorized activities

### 5.7 Software and Licensing Violations
- Installing or using unlicensed, pirated, or unauthorized software
- Using software outside the scope of its license agreement
- Distributing company-licensed software to unauthorized individuals
- Modifying, reverse engineering, or tampering with licensed software
- Using company resources to develop software for personal or external use without approval

## 6. Electronic Communications and Collaboration
- Company email, messaging, and collaboration systems are for business communication
- Professional, respectful, and courteous communication expected in all interactions
- No reasonable expectation of privacy in company communication systems
- Monitoring may occur for security, maintenance, compliance, or investigative purposes
- Automatic forwarding of company email to personal accounts prohibited without approval
- Use of personal email accounts for company business requires authorization and compliance
- Retention of electronic communications as per applicable policies and legal requirements

## 7. Internet and Network Usage
- Web browsing for legitimate business purposes is permitted and encouraged
- Access to job-related websites, research resources, and professional networks supported
- Personal internet use permitted during designated breaks and outside work hours
- Web filtering and content controls may be employed to protect against threats
- Downloading files or software requires security scanning and may require IT approval
- Bandwidth-intensive activities may be limited or scheduled during off-peak hours
- Use of company resources for illegal file sharing or copyright infringement prohibited

## 8. Mobile Devices and Remote Work
### 8.1 Company-Owned Devices
- Subject to all provisions of this acceptable use policy
- May be monitored for security, compliance, and proper use
- Must be returned immediately upon termination or upon request
- Lost or stolen devices must be reported to IT security within one hour
- Personal use permitted but must comply with policy limitations

### 8.2 Bring Your Own Device (BYOD)
- Personal devices used for company work must be registered and approved
- Must comply with company security requirements (encryption, passwords, updates)
- Company may require installation of security profiles or management software
- Company data on personal devices must be protected as if on company premises
- Remote wipe capability may be enabled for lost/stolen devices or policy violations
- Separation of company and personal data recommended through containerization or profiles

### 8.3 Remote and Telework
- Remote access must use approved secure methods (VPN, zero-trust network access, etc.)
- Company data accessed remotely must be treated with same protections as onsite
- Home networks used for company work must meet minimum security standards
- Equipment used for remote work must be maintained in secure condition
- Ergonomic and safety considerations apply to home office setups

## 9. Social Media and Online Presence
### 9.1 Personal Use
- Personal social media use during work hours limited to designated breaks
- Employees are personally responsible for content they post on personal accounts
- Personal posts should not interfere with job performance or workplace relationships
- Employees should be mindful of how personal activity reflects on professional reputation

### 9.2 Company-Affiliated Accounts
- Creation and use of social media accounts representing the company requires authorization
- Authorized users must follow company branding, messaging, and engagement guidelines
- Confidential or non-public company information must not be disclosed on social media
- Political advocacy or personal opinions must not be presented as company positions
- Customer interactions on social media must follow company service guidelines

### 9.3 General Guidelines
- Employees must not represent themselves as speaking for the company without authorization
- Confidential company information must not be disclosed on any social media platform
- Respectful and professional communication expected in all company-related interactions
- Harassment, discrimination, or retaliation via social media is prohibited
- Intellectual property considerations apply to content shared on social media

## 10. Cloud Services and Third-Party Platforms
- Use of cloud services for company business requires appropriate authorization
- Must comply with company data classification and security requirements
- Data stored in third-party clouds must be protected according to its classification
- Terms of service and acceptable use policies of third parties must be reviewed
- Integration with company systems requires security review and approval
- Regular assessment of third-party security and compliance practices required

## 11. Monitoring and Enforcement
- Company reserves right to monitor, log, and review usage of technology resources
- Monitoring may include network traffic, system logs, email content, and file access
- Monitoring conducted for security, maintenance, policy compliance, or investigative purposes
- Investigations conducted in accordance with applicable laws and company policies
- Violations may result in disciplinary action up to and including termination
- Illegal activities will be reported to appropriate law enforcement authorities
- Policy violations may result in loss of technology access privileges
- Repeated or severe violations may result in criminal or civil legal action

## 12. Policy Review and Updates
- This policy reviewed annually or as needed based on legal, technological, or business changes
- Updates communicated to all workforce members with appropriate training provided
- Version control maintained; superseded versions archived for reference
- Feedback on policy effectiveness and clarity encouraged and reviewed
